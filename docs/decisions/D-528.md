## D-528 · Lab readiness is measured: a flag's card says when it is ready to publish
**Date:** 2026-11-02 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** extends D-498 (runtime flags) and D-480 (🚀 Publicar para todos); keeps D-395 (counting is itself behind the lab) · **Topics:** structure, panel
**Context:** features behind `flag('<id>')` stayed in *lab* until the owner remembered to try them; nothing said "this one has been used enough and nothing broke" (#1089, automation 4/5).
**Decision:** when a flag turns on for a lab key holder, the load counts in `labsUso/<id>/<day>`: loads, finished loads (the page's `flagDone(id)`, or 30 s open and closed without a vigía failure), loads with a failure, and the first 8 hex of each key's SHA-256 (aggregate only, no player data). The Mac mini's hourly `lab-ready` job (`tools/agents/lab-ready.mjs check`) weighs them, since the flag was last switched, against `READY` in `flag-list.js` (≥5 finished, ≥2 keys, ≥3 days, 0 errors; `READY_BY` overrides per flag) and writes `flagsListas/<id>`; the flag's `/labs/` card shows **✅ Listo para 🚀** with the numbers, or what is missing. The processes round tells the owner once (`lab-ready.mjs news`). The owner still presses 🚀. The counting itself is behind its flag `lab-ready`.
**Why:** publishing should rest on evidence, not on the owner's memory; the owner keeps the last step.
**Consequences:** `firebase/lab-ready.test.mjs` checks the rules, `tools/agents/lab-ready.test.mjs` the threshold math. A flagged page that wants a precise "finished" calls `flagDone(id)` from `lab-usage.js`.
