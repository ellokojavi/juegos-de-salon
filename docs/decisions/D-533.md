## D-533 · The design system: one visual language, owned by the usability agent
**Date:** 2026-11-05 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** amends C-1 (shared palette and classes) with the scales and the owner; extends D-132 (the usability agent) and D-520 (what a program can measure, a program checks); D-309 (board air) keeps its tokens · **Topics:** identity, agents
**Context:** C-1 asked every game to take buttons, panels, chips and titles from `base.css`, and the palette and fonts were respected. But there was no scale for distances, corners or shadows, no second-level components, and nothing that checked a stylesheet: the 26 game stylesheets held 325 hex colours written by hand (148 distinct ones that were not a token), 338 hand-written radii against 11 with a token, and the same row, toggle or link button copied into four to six games with different paddings. The owner asked on 2026-11-05 for a design system the usability agent designs and keeps alive, migrated in phases that can be checked one by one (#1188).
**Decision:**
- **The design system exists** and lives in two places only: `docs/DESIGN.md` (the rules, the tokens' meaning, the components, the escapes, the migration recipe) and `public/assets/css/base.css` (the tokens and the shared classes; `ranking.css` for rankings).
- **The scales are tokens on `:root`**, named by value where the value is the meaning: space on a 2 px grid up to 20 then steps of 4 (`--s-2` … `--s-32`), corners by value (`--r-8`, `--r-10`, `--r-12`, `--r-14`, `--r-16`, `--r-22`, `--r-pill`; `--radius` stays as the alias of `--r-22`); the edges under buttons (`--edge-pink/yellow/cyan`) and the text on yellow and cyan (`--ink-on-yellow/cyan`); `--shadow-panel`, `--shadow-text`; text steps in hundredths of a rem (`--fs-75` … `--fs-200`). The scales were drawn from `base.css` and widened once with the games measured by the usability agent (18 px space, 10 and 14 px corners, 0.9, 1.1 and 1.4 rem), so most of what games write today maps to a step without a visible change; a new step after this is a decision, not an edit.
- **The usability agent owns it:** the document, the design lint (phase 2, `tools/agents/design-lint.mjs`, with its allowlist; a ratchet that never let a file's count grow until phase 6 made the lint a gate) and the components page (phase 3, `/labs/design/`). It reviews what the lint cannot measure (D-520): meaning of a colour, the right title step, whether a new control should be a component.
- **Migration is gradual** (#1188): foundations, lint in report mode, components page, second-level components with Ahorcado as pilot, then one game per PR (La Copa last, in two PRs), and only then the lint becomes a gate. Each PR leaves every test green and lists its intended visual differences with screenshots at 390 and 320 px.
**Why:** The owner's request, and the measurement: the parts of C-1 that had a token were respected and the parts that did not were not. A program can hold a scale; a reviewer cannot hold 374 paddings in their head. Phases, because a big-bang CSS migration is the kind of change that breaks a game without anyone noticing, and because each phase adds the check that proves the previous one holds.
**Consequences:** This PR (phase 1) adds the tokens and uses them inside `base.css` where it wrote the same values by hand, so the computed styles of every screen are unchanged (checked on 13 screens at 390 and 320 px). `docs/DESIGN.md` is in CLAUDE.md's table and in the usability agent's brief. C-1 points at it. The following phases are the sub-issues of #1188. Phase 6 (2026-11-05): with every stylesheet at 0 hits not allowlisted, the lint is a gate in `design-lint.test.mjs` (the `tests` check) and `design-lint.mjs --gate`; the ratchet and `docs/design-baseline.json` are gone.
