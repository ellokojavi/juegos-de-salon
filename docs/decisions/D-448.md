## D-448 · ☕ Buy me a coffee: voluntary support through a Stripe Payment Link, quiet and in two places (in the lab)
**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Status:** amended by D-449, D-458 · **Relation:** — · **Topics:** home, panel
**Context:** The owner wants players who enjoy the games to be able to support them with a small
donation, in the spirit of "Buy me a coffee", and asked whether Stripe is the right provider and
where the invitation should go: after a game, as an icon that is always visible, or something else.
The money goes to a US account. The site is static (GitHub Pages), so a provider that needs a
server was out.
**Decision:** Stripe **Payment Links**, with "customers choose what to pay": a hosted checkout URL,
no backend, Apple Pay and Google Pay, and no platform fee on top of card processing (Buy Me a
Coffee adds 5%, Ko-fi takes players off to its own page, PayPal is clumsy on a phone). The link
lives in one constant, `PAYMENT_LINK` in `public/assets/js/support.js`, and Stripe's confirmation
page sends the donor back to the support page with `?thanks`. The invitation shows in two quiet places,
never during a game and never as a popup (as the inbox, U-38):
- **The home page footer**, always, under "💬 Suggestions and comments": whoever wants to support
  always knows where.
- **The end of a finished game**, after everything else, with one sentence ("Juegos de Salón is free and has
  no ads. If you enjoy it, you can support it.") and the link, with its own rule on the device: not
  before the device's 6th finished game, at most once every 7 days, never on an ending that already shows
  the inbox link (one quiet link per ending), 30 days of rest after opening the support page,
  and never again after paying.
- **The support page** (`/labs/support/` while in the lab, `/support/` when it leaves it): what
  the money is for, that the games stay free whether one pays or not, that Stripe handles the
  card, and one button. (D-449 made it a card over the page instead.)
There is no permanent icon in the top bar and no splash: the top bar is the game's tools (share,
rankings, language, sound), and a coffee there on every screen asks every visit, including the
first, before the player has enjoyed anything. A splash campaign can announce it once when it
leaves the lab, through the usual queue (D-351, D-430).
Every step is counted in the day's stats as `support/<event>` (`shown`, `open`, `pay`, `thanks`),
only as counts: the funnel says whether the end-of-game invitation works or annoys.
**Why:** Donations come from people at the moment they enjoyed something, which is the end of a
game, but an invitation that comes every time becomes noise and then irritation; the inbox
already showed that a rule kept on the device (D-345) keeps a quiet link quiet. The texts never say
"donation" or imply a tax deduction: the payment is support to a person, not to a charity.
They speak in the first person, at the owner's request ("Invítame a un café", "Buy me a coffee"): one
person making the games is asking, which reads warmer than a brand asking. The inbox keeps its "Escríbenos".
**Consequences:** In the lab (D-395, D-409): every place asks `labAccess()` and the lab lists it
(`#seccion-apoyo`). Until the owner sets `PAYMENT_LINK`, the button simulates the payment
(`?thanks&test`, nothing counted or remembered). To leave the lab: set the link, move the page to
`/support/` (and Stripe's confirmation URL with it), drop the `labAccess()` checks, and show the
`support/` counters in the panel.
