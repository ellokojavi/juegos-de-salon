## D-460 · ¿Dónde queda?'s Earth in WebP, next to the game, and the 4096 px image only on big screens
**Date:** 2026-10-27 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** amends D-159 (which images are downloaded, and in what format), D-160 and D-201 (the tiles' format and folder) · **Topics:** cup, tests
**Context:** The 2026-10-27 audit (#834, #846) found that every visit to `/where/` started downloading
both whole Earth images, the 2048 px JPEG (462 KB) and the 4096 px one (1.6 MB), though the globe uses
only one: 2.1 MB of images before a phone placed its first pin, and 2.7 MB zoomed in over a city. The 128
tiles weighed 15 MB in JPEG. And no page named them in a way the end-to-end scope could follow
(`${w}` in the path), so touching them ran every script.
**Decision:**
- **The 4096 px image only where it shows.** The 2048 one is always asked for first. The 4096 one is asked
  for only when the globe's canvas is wider than 2048 device pixels and the video card takes a 4096
  texture (`satelite()` in `globo.js`); then it replaces the 2048 one when it arrives, as before. The
  tiles start at the same zoom as before (where the 4096 one would fall short), also on a phone that has
  only the 2048 one, so a phone at rest downloads no tiles. The 4 s cap and the vector map fallback
  (D-245) are unchanged: the cap starts with the first request.
- **WebP**, written once from NASA's original by `node tools/generators/map.mjs satellite [<nasa.jpg>]`
  (all with PIL in `tools/generators/tiles.py`; quality 82 for the whole images, 78 for the tiles). The
  JPEGs are gone.
- **Next to the game**: `public/cup/games/where/earth-2004-09/` holds `2048.webp`, `4096.webp` and the
  tiles `row-column.webp`. `globo.js` names the folder with `new URL('earth-2004-09/', import.meta.url)`,
  and `tools/e2e/affected.mjs` reads that as a load of every file under it (a folder that is not a page);
  a deleted site file that nothing names any more narrows nothing instead of running every script.
**Why:** The issue's target: a phone's visit to `/where/` downloads under 1 MB of images. Measured with
Chrome on the local site, before → after: a phone (390 × 844 @3) 2,088 KB → 203 KB; the same phone zoomed
in over Chile 2,679 KB → 622 KB; a desktop (1280 × 800 @2) 2,088 KB → 877 KB. The whole images are about
55–60 % lighter at the same look, the tiles about 35 % (15 MB → 9.6 MB). Every browser with WebGL reads
WebP (Safari since 14).
**Consequences:** A browser without WebP (Safari 13 and older) gets the vector map, as one without WebGL
does. A change to the Earth's images runs only La Copa's and ¿Dónde queda?'s scripts. Turning the splash
GIF clips into video, the issue's third point, is left for later.
