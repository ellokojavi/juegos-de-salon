## D-445 · Stack: a one-tap tower game in Juegos en prueba, in English
**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** follows D-280 and D-361 (the lab's groups), D-304 and D-413 (a lab-only PR) · **Topics:** stack
**Context:** the owner sent a video of Stack (Ketchapp, 2016) and asked for an evaluation: one of the best
one-tap games, quick and with a clear number to beat, but pure timing, the opposite of the house's
"deducible without guessing" puzzles, and hard to compare fairly across phones. Then: "Build the
prototype directly and put it in Labs. English only for now".
**Decision:** a solo prototype at `/labs/stack/`, in 🎲 Juegos en prueba, credited on the cover and the lab
card, in English only. A block slides over the tower, a tap drops it, the overhang is cut off; a drop
within 3.5% is perfect and cuts nothing, and from 4 perfects in a row each one grows the block back; a
miss ends the game; the score is the height, and the best is kept on the phone. Every game starts the
same: no clock and no daily board. A drop is judged at the tap's own timestamp against a slide that
depends only on time, so the frame rate does not change the game. Details in `docs/games/stack.md`.
**Why:** the owner's request; the site has no reflex game, and a 30-second one is a different thing to
try. Fairness is built in from the start because a score that may one day go to La Copa or a ranking
must not depend on the phone.
**Alternatives discarded:** going through the game-ideas agent first (the owner asked to build it
directly); Spanish only, as lab prototypes usually are (the owner asked for English); a daily seeded
tower (every run plays nearly the same, so a seed would only cap the tries); a 3D library (a 2D canvas
with isometric boxes is enough, and the site has no build).
**Consequences:** the first lab game in English only; `engine.test.mjs` and `tools/e2e/stack/lab.mjs`
cover it. What would prove it: players who play more than one round in a visit.
