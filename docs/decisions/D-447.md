## D-447 · Dudo: shake the phone to roll, and a turn clock that costs a die
**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** builds on D-188 and D-374 (the random-game die) and D-70 (dice committed with a hash) · **Topics:** touch
**Context:** the owner's request: in Dudo, getting your dice should take the real table's gesture,
shaking the cup, and a player should have a fixed time to answer or lose a die. It was built in the
lab (D-395, D-409) over several rounds the owner reviewed on the preview and then on a real phone:
the dice had to roll visibly while the phone shakes, in a panel of their own with bigger dice and
no yellow ace, keep rolling as long as the shaking lasts, and stop as they fell when it stops, with
no second animation. The owner tried it on a phone and asked to publish it.
**Decision:** two setup switches in every mode but Uno al día (which keeps DEFAULT_CONFIG, both off):
- **🫨 Agitar para tirar** (`config.shake`), **on by default.** While your dice are still to roll,
  the hand's panel is a green cloth with 64 px dice face down. Each jolt (13 m/s² beyond gravity)
  spins 3D dice (`dado3d.js` with pip faces) harder, the phone vibrates with them, and they keep
  rolling while the shaking lasts. After at least three jolts and 350 ms of stillness each die runs
  out its own spin and stops on the nearest pose ahead that shows its face; the hand keeps the turn
  it landed with, and the cloth shrinks back into the hand. No sensor, no WebGL or a refused
  iPhone permission: a 🎲 button (or flat dice) does the same. No bid until the dice are out.
- **⏱️ 30 segundos por turno** (`config.turnSecs`), **off by default.** The player on turn has 30 s
  (rolling included) to bid, doubt or call it exactly; out of time their own phone sends `timeout`
  and they lose a die, the table is not revealed, and they open the next round. The clock pauses
  under a reveal, a pass screen or a hidden page (U-20).
In a room the creator's switches travel in the config to every phone.
**Why:** the shake turns the most physical moment of the real game into the app's own, and on by
default is how the owner meant it: the game is the cup. It changes nothing in the engine or the
fairness: the dice are still rolled with the browser's chance before the shake (and committed with
their hash in a room, C-10, D-70), and the shake only shows them; letting the resting pose pick the
value would favour some faces (with these two angles, 2 and 5 would come out twice as often). The
clock does change the rules (a die can be lost without a call), so each table chooses it.
**Consequences:** every Dudo round starts with a shake unless the switch is off; the e2e scripts
that play whole games turn it off, and `tools/e2e/liars-dice/shake-clock.mjs` covers the shake and
the clock. In a room the clock runs only on the phone whose turn it is: a player who leaves or hides
the app still stalls the table, as before. An iPhone does not vibrate (Safari has no vibration API).
