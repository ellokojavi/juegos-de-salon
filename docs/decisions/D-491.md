## D-491 · Dudo's fives are "quinas", and the obligada notice names its exception
**Date:** 2026-10-28 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** amends D-71, D-490 · **Topics:** modes
**Context:** D-71 named Dudo's faces as said at a Chilean table, and wrote the fives as "quintas". The
owner corrected it: in Chile the fives are **"quina" / "quinas"**, not "quinta" / "quintas". Oreste
Plath's *Origen y folclor de los juegos en Chile* agrees ("los 5 son quinas o chincoles"). Separately,
the short notice of the open obligada round (D-490) said "la pinta no cambia" without the exception
the long text gives: a player who also has one die may change it.
**Decision:** The owner's rule: the fives are "quina" (one) and "quinas" (many), for everyone, wherever
the player reads the Chilean names (the bid, the hint, the rules, the cover and its FAQ). The other
names of D-71 (ases, tontos, trenes, cuadras, sextas) stay until the owner decides on them. The short
obligada notice now carries its exception in the four languages ("…la pinta no cambia, salvo quien
también tiene un dado"). In the open round, your own hidden die in the rivals' strip is drawn as a
die-sized ❓, the same size as the rivals' dice, instead of a small character.
**Why:** The names are only worth having if they are the ones the table uses; a wrong one reads as
foreign. A short notice that drops the exception tells the one-die players a rule that is false for them.
**Consequences:** "quinta" stays where it means "fifth" (frases.js, "De la quinta, todos"). The lab
gating of the classic rules (D-490) does not change; the quina fix is not gated, it is a wording fix.
