## D-400 · The merge no longer waits two minutes for the app's monitor; sessions wait with wait-pr.mjs
**Date:** 2026-10-20 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** amends D-323 (the hold) and D-218 (the monitor as the way to learn of the merge); extended by D-441 (a REST fallback and `unreadable` after five failed reads) · **Topics:** agents, publishing
**Context:** the last step of the required `end-to-end` check held the check 120 s after commenting "both
checks are green", so the app's PR monitor would read the comment before the PR closed (D-323). Every PR
with auto-merge merged two minutes later for it, and the monitor is not available in every session: a
cloud session could not turn it on and learned of each merge with a background `gh` loop. The owner
agreed to drop the hold.
**Decision:** `tools/release/merge-report.mjs --ready` still comments, but no longer holds the check.
A new `tools/agents/wait-pr.mjs <n>`, run in the background after turning on auto-merge, asks GitHub every
minute and exits on `merged`, `closed`, `failed: <check>` (only `tests` or `end-to-end`, the required
ones) or `conflict`. `CLAUDE.md` (point 5 of "Before proposing a merge") and C-11 in `CANONES.md` say so.
The app's monitor may still be turned on for review comments.
**Why:** a wait in the session costs no tokens and no one else's time; a wait in the check cost every PR
two minutes, for a signal only some sessions could receive.
**Consequences:** each auto-merged PR merges about two minutes sooner. A session that does not run
`wait-pr.mjs` and relies only on the monitor may learn of the merge late or not at all; nothing is lost
(publishing and cleanup do not depend on it). `wait-pr.test.mjs` checks what it reports.
