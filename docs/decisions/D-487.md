## D-487 · Dudo's traditional rules: no opening in aces and the obligada round, in the lab
**Date:** 2026-10-28 · **Status:** current · **Relation:** completes D-69 (which left the obligada round for later) · **Topics:** modes
**Context:** Issue #897 asked to audit Dudo against the traditional Chilean rules. The audit (comment on #897) found that aces as wild, halving to go into aces, double plus one to leave them, and calzar were already there. Two rules of the Chilean table were missing: a round is not opened in aces, and the obligada round (D-69 said it would come, and left the engine ready for it).
**Decision:** One setup switch, **🧪 Classic rules** (`config.traditional`), off by default and offered only to lab key holders (`labAccess()`, D-395, D-409; card `#seccion-dudo-tradicional` in `/labs/`). With it on: (1) nobody opens a round in aces (going to aces later, at half, is still allowed); (2) the first time a player is down to one die, the round they open is **obligada**: aces are not wild, and everyone keeps the pinta the opener named, only raising the quantity. The opener may name aces there. Each player gets one obligada round per game. Calzar keeps its usual conditions in it. The bot follows the same rules. In a room the creator's choice travels in the config.
**Why:** both rules are part of the game as it is played in Chile, and neither contradicts a decision in force: D-69 only postponed the obligada round. They go to the lab so today's players keep today's rules until the owner tries them. What varies from table to table (obligada with two players left, calzar during obligada, seeing only your own die, a calza that also gives a die back when you have five) is asked of the owner in #897, not built.
**Consequences:** `minBid` and `bidOk` take a `rules` argument (`{ obligada, noAceOpen }`), with `Infinity` as "not allowed"; `buildState` returns `rules` and `obligada` and marks `last.obligada`, so the reveal counts aces only as aces. Without the switch nothing changes, and saved games resume the same.
